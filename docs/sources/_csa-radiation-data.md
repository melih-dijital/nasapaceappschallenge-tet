# CSA Data: Monitoring Radiation for Astronaut Health

## Article Metadata
- **Title:** Monitoring radiation for astronaut health
- **Publisher:** Canadian Space Agency (CSA)
- **Published Date:** 2026-02-26
- **URL:** [Canadian Space Agency](https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/news/articles/2026/2026-02-26-monitoring-radiation-astronaut-health.asp)
- **Language:** English

## Mission Context
- **Current Testbed:** International Space Station (ISS)
- **Future Destinations:** Moon, Mars
- **Key Challenge:** Radiation exposure beyond Earth's atmosphere

## Project Details
- **Project Name:** CANS
- **Project Full Name:** Canadian Active Neutron Spectrometer
- **Contractor:** Bubble Technology Industries
- **Contract Value:** $5,500,000 CAD
- **Instrument Type:** Compact and autonomous instrument
- **Purpose:** Measure neutron radiation exposure in space
- **Key Features:**
  - Works continuously
  - Works autonomously

## Radiation Facts
- **Type:** Neutron radiation
- **ISS Radiation Share:** 30% of total radiation
- **Impacts:** Serious biological damage
- **Mitigation Needs:** Specialized protective measures

## Historical Context
- **Duration of ISS Testing:** 25 years
- **Previous Canadian Experiments:** 
  - MOSFET
  - EVARM
  - Radi-N
  - Radi-N2
  - Bubble detectors

## Earth Applications
The technologies and data gathered have the following applications on Earth:
- Cancer therapy
- Radiation protection for aircrews
- Nuclear threat detection
- Public safety
